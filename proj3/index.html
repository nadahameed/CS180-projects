Fun with Flow Models

In this project, I experimented with image generation and editing using flow models. It was fun experimenting with different image modification techniques and seeing what the models came up with.

My general takeaways from this project were first of all, how easy it was to use a flow model. It was fun experimenting with different prompts, and while true art can never be replaced, it was fun to see the visualization steps and the process. And then the difference between single step denoising and flow matching was very interesting to me. The results have stark differences, and it was cool to see different modifications and improvements we could make to our flow model to strengthen it.

Part 2: Conditional Generation and Guidance

Part 2.1: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

Classifier-free guidance pushes velocity predictions toward prompt-specific features, away from more general features. In this part, I implemented classifier-free guidance in the hopes of improving prompt alignment and image quality. But it reduces diversity. Compared to section 1.6, the generated images look a lot more cohesive—there aren't any visible flaws or oddities. The faces do look a lot more similar to each other, dark hair, dark eyes, pale skin, but even beyond that, there's just an odd underlying similarity between them.

Based on the output results of the model, you can see that the model is just generating a blurred image/output. It is kind of computing the average of all the digits, and not really doing the diversification/ identification we may typically expect. This may be happening because we are minimizing over L2 loss, which means the model is unsure of a specific number/shape, and instead computes the average, to minimize loss for all the digits.


Part 2: Training a Flow Matching Model

We saw that one-step denoising didn't work too well. Instead, I implemented flow matching, which denoises an image by training a UNet model to predict flow from noisy to clean.

Part 2.1 and 2.2: Adding Time to the UNet and Training the UNet

Now we are predicting flow, so I injected time into the UNet model to condition it, using the FCBlock operator. Then, I trained it by picking a random image from the training set, picking a random timestep t, adding noise to the original image, and then training the denoiser to predict the flow at that output. And then I repeated this for multiple images and timesteps until the model converged.
