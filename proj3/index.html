Fun with Flow Models

In this project, I experimented with image generation and editing using flow models. It was fun experimenting with different image modification techniques and seeing what the models came up with.


Part A: Exploring the Power of Flow Models

Part 1: Visualizing Training and Sampling

Part 1.1: Constructing Noisy Training Inputs

In this part, I implemented a function to interpolate between a clean image and Gaussian noise. I added noise and scaled the image. At t=0, there is noise, and at t=1, there is the clean image. I sampled a Gaussian noise tensor and reused it to construct noisy images at different t. Here is a bird with noise.


Part 1.2: Classical Denoising

For those noisy images I came up with in the previous part, I applied Gaussian denoising, here are the attempts. Not bad.





Part 1.3: One-Step Denoising

Here I used a pretrained flow model to denoise. From the noisy images from previous steps, I used the model to do a one step estimate.





Part 1.4: Visualizing Velocity

To estimate the clean image, I added (1 - t) times the velocity to the clean image. Here, I show the error between the model and ground truth updates.





Part 1.5: Iterative Denoising with Euler Sampler

One step denoising can be inaccurate because the learned velocity changes as we move along the path. In this part, I used Euler integration to predict a new velocity at every small step, making multiple denoiser calls for the same image.




Part 1.6: Generating Image from Noise

The Euler sampling function can also generate images from scratch. Given the prompt "a high quality photo," these were the generated outputs by the model for certain seeds, aka denoisings of pure noise.



Part 2: Conditional Generation and Guidance

Part 2.1: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

Classifier-free guidance pushes velocity predictions toward prompt-specific features, away from more general features. In this part, I implemented classifier-free guidance in the hopes of improving prompt alignment and image quality. But it reduces diversity. Compared to section 1.6, the generated images look a lot more cohesive—there aren't any visible flaws or oddities. The faces do look a lot more similar to each other, dark hair, dark eyes, pale skin, but even beyond that, there's just an odd underlying similarity between them.
